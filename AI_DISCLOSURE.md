# AI Usage & Disclosure

## 1. Overview

**Interplanetary Survival Map**, developed by Team Quanta Buddies for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, is an interactive Mars exploration and Marswalk planning platform.

The project combines NASA-related scientific datasets, conventional software systems, deterministic computational methods, and AI-assisted capabilities to support exploration, geological research, and mission planning.

This document explains how Artificial Intelligence (AI) is used within the project and distinguishes AI-generated outputs from conventional application logic and scientific data.

## 2. AI-Assisted Development

AI tools were used during development to support:

- Code generation and debugging.
- UI/UX development and refinement.
- Software architecture and component organization.
- Documentation and technical writing.
- Identifying potential performance and compatibility issues.

All AI-assisted code and suggestions are subject to developer review, modification, and testing.

## 3. AI Technologies and Integration

The application uses Google Gemini-based AI capabilities where configured in the project.

AI integration is intended to support conversational interaction, contextual explanations, and assistance with Mars exploration workflows.

Exact model identifiers and service configurations depend on the deployed implementation and should be verified against the actual application source code.

No unpublished model names, integrations, or capabilities are claimed in this disclosure.

## 4. ARES Commander — Unified AI Voice Assistant

ARES Commander is the project's unified AI assistant.

Its intended capabilities include:

- Natural-language interaction.
- Voice-based communication and spoken responses.
- Context-aware assistance related to Mars exploration.
- Explaining available scientific and mission information.
- Assisting users in navigating and understanding application features.

AI-generated responses are informational and should not be interpreted as authoritative scientific conclusions or validated operational instructions.

The assistant does not independently establish astronaut safety or guarantee mission success.

## 5. AI-Assisted Decision Support

Where implemented, AI may assist users by interpreting available information and generating contextual explanations.

Potential application areas include:

- Explaining terrain and geological information.
- Summarizing identified exploration targets.
- Communicating potential route considerations.
- Providing understandable explanations of mission-planning information.

AI-generated interpretations are advisory. They are not equivalent to independently verified scientific measurements.

## 6. Deterministic Algorithms and Conventional Software

Not every intelligent-looking feature in the application uses AI.

The project also uses conventional programming, mathematical calculations, and deterministic algorithms.

Examples include:

- A* Pathfinding for route computation.
- Waypoint processing.
- Elevation and terrain-data visualization.
- Distance and coordinate calculations.
- Rule-based hazard evaluation, where implemented.
- Mission sequencing and simulation logic.

These operations should be attributed to their underlying algorithms and application logic rather than described as AI-generated results.

## 7. Scientific Data and Sources

The application incorporates Mars-related scientific and geospatial information from NASA-associated missions and datasets, depending on the availability and implementation of each data source.

Scientific observations, processed datasets, derived calculations, and AI-generated explanations are distinct types of information.

The interface should clearly distinguish:

- **NASA / Mission Data:** Original or processed scientific observations.
- **Derived Data:** Information calculated from available datasets.
- **Simulation:** Values and scenarios generated by application logic.
- **AI Output:** Explanations or interpretations generated by an AI model.

Historical or cached scientific datasets must not be represented as live observations or real-time NASA mission telemetry.

## 8. Limitations and Responsible Use

Interplanetary Survival Map is an educational and research-oriented prototype.

- AI-generated content may contain inaccuracies.
- Scientific interpretations require independent verification.
- Route recommendations are not certified astronaut navigation instructions.
- Simulated mission conditions do not represent actual Martian environmental conditions.
- The application does not guarantee astronaut safety or mission success.

Users should independently verify important scientific and operational information using authoritative sources.

## 9. Transparency

Team Quanta Buddies is committed to transparency regarding the use of AI in this project.

AI is used as an assistance and interpretation layer, not as a replacement for scientific evidence, verified datasets, deterministic calculations, or human judgment.

The project does not claim that every feature is powered by AI.
